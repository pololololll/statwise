About the project

Making statistical analysis easier and more accessible

StatWise is a web-based statistical analysis tool that lets you enter a sample of data and get clear results with a plain-language explanation. Its name says what it is for: Analyze your data, Understand what the numbers mean, and Decide what to conclude.

Every analysis you run is saved to your account's history in this browser, so you can review or reopen it later. The Module 7 and Module 9 pages hold the course PDFs for reference while you work.

Scope

What StatWise can do

  • Simple linear regression — regression equation (y′ = A + Bx), slope, intercept, Pearson's r, R², a scatter plot with the regression line, and prediction of Y for a given X.
  • Strength of correlation — interpreted with the Module 7 rule of thumb (very high, high, moderate, low, negligible).
  • Hypothesis testing — one-sample and two-sample Z-tests and t-tests, with α of 0.10, 0.05 or 0.01 and two-tailed, right-tailed or left-tailed alternatives.
  • Critical-value method — test statistic, critical value, rejection region, degrees of freedom, a decision, and a bell-curve diagram.
  • Learning support — sample data for both tools, analysis history, and the Module 7 and Module 9 PDFs.
  • Light and dark mode — your choice is remembered.
Limitations

What to keep in mind

  • Educational use. StatWise is a student project for learning and practice. Double-check important results before using them in graded or professional work.
  • Regression is simple only. It handles one predictor (X) and one response (Y), and assumes a straight-line relationship. It does not offer multiple regression, significance tests or confidence intervals for the slope, or residual diagnostics.
  • Correlation is not causation. Predictions outside the range of your X values (extrapolation) can be unreliable.
  • Hypothesis tests use summary statistics. You enter means, standard deviations and sample sizes — not raw data. Results are by the critical-value method; p-values are not computed.
  • Two-sample t-test degrees of freedom use df = min(n₁ − 1, n₂ − 1), the smaller sample's df, as taught in the module. Other methods (such as Welch's formula) would give slightly different critical values.
  • Limited options. Only Z- and t-tests, only three significance levels, and no paired or proportion tests, ANOVA or chi-square.
  • Data stays in your browser. Accounts and history are stored only on this device. There is no server, so nothing syncs between devices, the sign-in is not meant to protect sensitive data, and clearing browser data erases it.
